Supermarkets, food, and the Pfand economy

Grocery stores, Sunday closures, bottle deposits, waste sorting, markets, Mensa, cafés, and Berlin cuisine as a genuinely plural form.

Saturday advice

If Sunday food matters, buy it before Sunday. Most regular shops close on Sundays and public holidays.

Sunday

The closure principle

Most shops close on Sundays and public holidays. Exceptions include some station shops, airport shops, petrol stations, pharmacies on duty, bakeries during permitted hours, tourist/specialty exceptions, and official Sunday openings.

Translation: buy groceries on Saturday unless you already have a station-shop plan.

Pfand

Bottles with afterlives

Many bottles and cans carry a deposit. Return them at supermarket machines, take the receipt, and redeem it at checkout. If you leave deposit bottles next to a public bin, someone may collect them; this is part of Berlin’s informal recycling economy.

Handbook Germany: Pfand and recycling
Waste sorting

Waste sorting

Expect paper/cardboard, packaging, organic waste, residual waste, and separate glass containers. The exact setup depends on your building. When in doubt, ask a neighbour; this may be the fastest path to practical local knowledge.

Food and drink

Berlin cuisine is plural

Döner and currywurst are canonical, but Berlin’s food culture is also Vietnamese restaurants, Turkish bakeries, Syrian sweets, vegan experiments, Kantstraße noodles, market halls, natural wine bars, lake beer, Kneipe regulars, coffee-and-cake, and Mensa pragmatism.

Markets and second-hand Berlin

Where shopping becomes leisure

Berlin’s weekly and flea markets are half retail, half theatre. Mauerpark, Boxhagener Platz, Fehrbelliner Platz, Maybachufer, Winterfeldtplatz, and the market halls are good starting points. Bring cash, curiosity, and a bag.
